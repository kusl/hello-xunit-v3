Life Feeling Too Expensive? It’s Not Just You. And It’s Not Just Inflation.
Affordability is expected to be a major issue for younger voters in the midterms. Here’s a snapshot of some reasons people are feeling pinched.
By Sabrina Tavernise, Eden Weingart, Nico Chilla, Georgia Gee and Steven Rich
Published 2026-10-22 01:09 UTC
https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/10/21/us/inflation-prices-real-estate-child-care-affordabilty.html

Your parents may talk about how they scrimped in their 20s. But the truth is, it probably wasn’t long before they owned a house. The median age of the first-time home buyer in 1987 was 29. Today, that first-time home buyer is 40.

Economists argue that, overall, life is more affordable now. Our wages are far higher and we pay far less for many basic goods than we did 40 years ago. But sober economic assessments don’t necessarily affect how people feel when they’re staring at the skyrocketing costs of the big items needed to achieve a middle-class life, like housing or education.

How exactly have these costs changed? Let’s take a closer look.

Housing

Housing is the financial and emotional center of many people’s lives. Prices have risen rapidly.

You can find similar increases in the price of homes throughout the country.

Child Care

For many families, child care is an essential expense. The modern economy is more or less built around two working parents. There again, costs have exploded.

Children’s activities have gotten more expensive too. When your parents went to the YMCA summer day camp in the 1970s and 1980s, it was cheap. Not anymore.

One week of summer day camp in Newark, Del.

Entertainment

OK, these next items are luxuries. But we included them because they make life fun, and because we remember going to a Van Halen concert in 1984 for $13.50 (about $45 in today’s dollars). In other words, middle-class people could afford them.

The Super Bowl is extreme. But what about something more basic, like camping, visiting an amusement park or skiing? A night of babysitting used to bring in enough money to cover a one-day ski pass. Not anymore.

Ski ticket in Breckenridge, Colo.

Single day pass to Disney World

Even camping in a national park, still relatively affordable, has more than doubled in price.

Camping in Yosemite at Bridalveil Creek

Education

Perhaps the single most important ingredient for a secure place in the American middle class is a college degree.

Health Care

Last but not least is health care. This critical component of modern life has had one of the biggest cost increases.

Why have prices gone up so much, well beyond the rate of inflation? Many complicated forces were behind each of these increases. With housing, for example, demand has gone up faster than supply. With child care, a labor-intensive industry, it has more to do with rising wages.

But don’t despair. The costs of some key things have gone down. Groceries, for example, used to take up a far bigger portion of our income. We also now spend far less for computers, clothes, toys and televisions than we did 40 years ago.
