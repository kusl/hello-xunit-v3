What Happens if Trump Doesn’t Like the Midterm Results?

Hope, dread and the final stretch to Election Day.

Oct. 24, 2026

By Michelle CottleJamelle Bouie and David French

Produced by Derek Arthur

We’re less than two weeks out from Election Day. In their last conversation before Americans head to the midterm polls, the national politics writer Michelle Cottle joins the columnists Jamelle Bouie and David French to take stock. While Jamelle makes the case for cautious optimism about a possible blue wave, David worries about what could happen if Trump doesn’t like the results.

Below is a transcript of an episode of “The Opinions.” We recommend listening to it in its original form for the full effect. You can do so using the player above or on the NYTimes app, Apple, Spotify, Amazon Music, YouTube, iHeartRadio or wherever you get your podcasts.

The transcript has been lightly edited for length and clarity.

Michelle Cottle: I’m Michelle Cottle. I cover national politics for New York Times Opinion, and I am here this week with my brilliant colleagues, columnists Jamelle Bouie and David French. Gentlemen, how goes it?

Jamelle Bouie: It goes well.

David French: Doing well, Michelle.

Cottle: All right. We are less than two weeks out from Election Day. This is our last episode before the will of the voters becomes clear, and I have so many pressing questions for you, most of which fall into the category of: What in God’s green earth is going to happen?
