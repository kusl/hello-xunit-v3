ARTICLE

Former Bard President With Epstein Ties Resigns From College Positions
Leon Botstein had already retired from the presidency of the liberal arts college this spring, after emails showed he had maintained a longtime friendship with Jeffrey Epstein.
By Vimal Patel
Published 2026-10-07 22:52 UTC
https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/07/us/leon-botstein-bard-college-resign-epstein.html

Leon Botstein, the former president of Bard College who had come under scrutiny for his ties to Jeffrey Epstein, resigned from all of his positions affiliated with the college on Wednesday.

The announcement came the same day that the House Judiciary Committee sent a letter to Bard leaders saying that while Dr. Botstein was president, he “likely enabled and possibly even participated in” Mr. Epstein’s abuse. The letter also raised concerns about how Bard under Dr. Botstein handled sexual-misconduct complaints on campus.

Dr. Botstein announced in the spring that he would retire from the presidency, a position he had held for half a century, but had stayed on in a variety of roles at the college until Wednesday. His plan to resign from other positions at the college was reported earlier by the Times Union.

The letter, from Representative Jamie Raskin, ranking member of the committee, pointed to an email from May 2014 in which a person writing from the Bard president’s email address had asked whether a woman Mr. Epstein may have been trafficking was “for me, or are you keeping her for yourself.”

“It is hard to construct any kind of innocent explanation for Botstein’s question and the surrounding events,” Mr. Raskin wrote, adding that the question was asked to “the most famous sexual predator of our time.”

In a statement, Dr. Botstein called the allegations in the letter untrue and said he did not write the message in question.

“Any assertion that I knew of or was involved in Epstein’s criminal behavior is wrong,” Dr. Botstein said. “I am confident that any fair review would confirm these facts. But I do not want to see even false allegations about me put the institution I have served for more than half a century put at any risk.”

He said he submitted his resignation to Bard’s board “to protect Bard from any distraction and ensure the college’s entire focus is on the future.”

The public redacted version of the email only lists the sender as “President,” but members of Congress have access to unredacted versions of the email. The letter from Mr. Raskin says the email was sent from “President@Bard.edu.”

Dr. Botstein was known during his long tenure on campus as a powerful fund-raiser, credited by some with keeping the college afloat during difficult financial times. But the release of a trove of Justice Department documents showed that he had a closer relationship with Mr. Epstein than previously believed.

The emails showed that Dr. Botstein exchanged visits and messages with Mr. Epstein for years. In a 2013 note, Dr. Botstein signed off with “Miss you,” and he wished the financier well after one news article that detailed his abuse.

Dr. Botstein has maintained that his relationship with Mr. Epstein was entirely about wooing a wealthy potential donor to give money to his college.

Bard commissioned a review of Dr. Botstein’s relationship with Mr. Epstein by the law firm WilmerHale, which raised concerns about the former president’s leadership but concluded that he had done nothing illegal. Mr. Raskin on Wednesday questioned the adequacy of the review.

In a statement, Brandon Weber, the chair of Bard College’s board of trustees, said that Dr. Botstein informed Bard on Wednesday that he was resigning immediately from all of his Bard positions, including his professorship and roles with the school’s music programs. The board unanimously accepted his resignation.

“Bard College is undertaking a full review of the allegations made in the House Judiciary Committee’s letter and cooperating with the committee’s investigation, as we have done from the outset,” Mr. Weber wrote.

Mr. Raskin’s letter also said that Dr. Botstein for most of his tenure as president personally oversaw decisions in cases related to Title IX, which prohibits sex discrimination, describing the arrangement as “highly irregular.”

Mr. Weber said that Dr. Botstein has not had unilateral decision-making authority over Title IX investigations “since the 2015-2016 school year.”

“Our message to everyone in the Bard community is simple: If you file a complaint, we will investigate it fairly, thoroughly and with the utmost respect for your safety,” Mr. Weber said.
