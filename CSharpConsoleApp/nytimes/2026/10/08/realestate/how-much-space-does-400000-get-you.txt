ARTICLE

How Much Space Does $400,000 Get You?
The median national home price gets you a vastly different spread, depending where you are.
By Arnesa A. Howell
Published 2026-10-08 09:00 UTC
https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/08/realestate/how-much-space-does-400000-get-you.html

Over the past six years, the national median home sales price has jumped over 20 percent to $400,000, according to a PropertyShark market study. Just how much space the median price gets you largely depends on — you guessed it — location.

PropertyShark analyzed the 100 largest U.S. cities to find that those in the Northeast and West offered the least space for the national median sale price. In Manhattan (New York City’s boroughs were considered individually in the report), a $400,000 budget would nab only 267 square feet, the least among all places studied. In Brooklyn one could expect 476 square feet. Of the 10 most expensive places, six were in California.

The study culled data from the U.S. census, PropertyShark sales data and other sources for its analysis, factoring in a mix of single-family homes, townhouses and condos.

“What these cities typically have in common are thriving economies, the major employment opportunities,” Laura Pop-Badiu, the author of the PropertyShark study, explained. Despite that $400,000 purchased only 393 square feet in San Francisco, Ms. Pop-Badiu said the city’s proximity to Silicon Valley attracted many professionals. Most downsize, the study found.

Comparatively, houses in the Midwest had the most floor space at the national median home price. The roomiest homes were in Detroit, where $400,000 could buy you a spacious, nearly 5,000-square-foot home. The cost: $81 a square foot.

While the national median sale price of $400,000 was the study’s point of comparison, in expensive markets, finding any home at that price is unlikely. In Manhattan, last year’s median sale price was far greater, $1.65 million, and the median home size, 1,100 square feet.
