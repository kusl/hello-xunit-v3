ARTICLE

The Best Cities to Retire in 2026
A study compared data across 45 metrics to find the best cities for senior living.
By Arnesa A. Howell
Published 2026-10-29 09:02 UTC
https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/29/realestate/the-best-cities-to-retire-in-2026.html

Florida emerged as the best state for retirees last year, according to a study by the personal finance platform WalletHub, and this year a similar study drilled down to the local level, pitting the 182 largest U.S. cities against one another. Four categories of data contributed equally to the final ranking: affordability, activities, quality of life and health care. Sources included the U.S. Census Bureau, the Bureau of Labor Statistics and WalletHub’s own research.

Once again, Florida was found attractive by retirees, with Orlando benefiting from the state’s favorable tax policies and handily ranking second for affordability, according to WalletHub. In fact, the three top retirement cities were all in Florida, and four made it into the top 10.

Ranked second and third, Miami and Tampa piggybacked on the state’s financial advantages for retirees, according to Chip Lupo, a WalletHub analyst. (With no state income tax, residents keep a larger chunk of their Social Security check and other retirement income. There are also no inheritance or estate taxes.) Orlando’s access to “very affordable” in-home health and adult day care services edged out the other two cities, Mr. Lupo explained. Overall, the city fared well in the health care category, offering older residents plenty of options for specialized care through gerontologists, quality geriatric hospitals and home health care facilities. A central geographic location helped, too, making it more weather-friendly than hurricane-prone Gulf Coast cities, Mr. Lupo continued.

Mild-weathered Scottsdale, Ariz., a city with the best quality of life and the second-highest share of people 65 and over, finished fourth overall — despite scoring 114th for affordability. Next was Casper, Wyo., cited as one of the cheapest cities to live in.

Among the five worst places to retire, four were in California: Stockton, Calif., came in last. New York City was least affordable.

“A lot of people do live on fixed incomes and don’t have a whole lot of flexibility,” Mr. Lupo said. “In that case, you may want to settle for maybe not one of the worst, but somewhere middle of the road, a place like Knoxville, Tenn., or Columbia, S.C.”
