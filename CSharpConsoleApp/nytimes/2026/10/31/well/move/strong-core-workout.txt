ARTICLE

For a Stronger Core, You Only Need 10 Minutes
Strengthening your midsection doesn’t have to take long, but it can have serious benefits.
By Jen Murphy and Theodore Tae
Published 2026-10-31 09:01 UTC
https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/31/well/move/strong-core-workout.html

You don’t need to put in hours at the gym to build a strong, stable core.

“When it comes to training the core, quality trumps quantity,” said Joel Sanders, director of performance at EXOS, a sports training center in Phoenix. “No one needs to do 100 crunches or feel a burn. Ten to 12 minutes of core work can do the trick.”

The core, which includes muscles along your back, the sides of your body and into your pelvis, has two main functions: to protect your spine by creating stability and to help transfer power from your lower body to your upper body. Exercises where you hold a position or do slow, controlled movements improve stability, while dynamic, full-body exercises such as rotational medicine ball slams help build power.

The core is “the epicenter of strength,” Mr. Sanders said. “All movement, from a lift to a lunge to a golf or tennis swing, is tied to the core.”

If you’re a new exerciser, you should approach your “core hygiene,” as Mr. Sanders calls it, from a 360-degree perspective — doing movements that hit the front, back and sides of your body, he said.

If you focus on good form, you don’t need to do more than six to 10 reps — or less than 30 seconds — of each exercise, he added. The reason, in part, is that your core is already getting worked by many daily activities, even sitting (with good posture). Also, since it’s made up of so many small muscles, you don’t need to train it with the same volume as the glutes or quads.

Doing too many repetitions can also lead to injuries, said Karl Erickson, a performance specialist at the Mayo Clinic in Rochester, Minn. Because your core — and particularly the deeper parts of it — acts like a corset for the spine, helping you maintain good posture, it’s particularly important to focus on form. When those muscles are fatigued, the spine can go from steal beam to wet noodle, Mr. Sanders said.

This core workout can be performed either as a standalone routine or as a warm-up before a workout, “to preheat the oven,” he said.

Overview

Time: 10 to 12 minutes

Intensity: Medium

What You’ll Need

- A mat
- A light to medium-weight dumbbell

Dumbbell Suitcase March

Purpose: Strengthens the abdominals, side muscles and glutes, as well as the shoulders and forearms

Time: 30 seconds

Pick up a weight and hold it in one hand, by your side. Keep your spine tall and your shoulders and hips square as you march in place, bringing your knees to hip height. Repeat with your other hand. To make it more challenging, hold the weight above your head with a straight arm.

Flutter Kicks

Purpose: Strengthens the abdominals, particularly the deep core and hip flexors

Time: 30 seconds

Lie face up with your legs straight and feet flexed. Place both hands either behind your head or palms down on either side of your hips for support. Draw your belly button toward your spine and use your abdominal muscles to lift your legs. Lift your legs, ideally three to six inches off the floor, but if you struggle to do that while keeping your back flat into the floor, it’s easier to lift them higher. Now flutter your legs, lifting your left, then your right and repeat.

Hip Rainbows

Purpose: Strengthens the abdominals, side muscles and shoulder stabilizers

Time: 30 seconds

Start in a forearm plank position with your elbows under your shoulders, and your knees, hips and torso in a straight line. Squeeze your glutes and engage your abdominals. Drop your right hip toward the floor, twisting your body to the right. Imagine drawing a rainbow with your hips as you bring your left hip up and over, then drop it toward the floor. Repeat for 30 seconds.

Beast Hold

Purpose: Strengthens the abdominals, side muscles and shoulder stabilizers

Time: 30 seconds

Start in a tabletop position, with your hands below your shoulders, your knees on the floor beneath your hips and your back flat. Engage your core as you lift and hover your knees one to three inches off the ground. The lower they are, the more difficult the exercise will be. Hold for 30 seconds. Bring your knees to the ground if you need a rest.

Plank Pull-Throughs

Purpose: Strengthens the abdominals, side muscles and shoulder stabilizers, shoulders, upper back

Time: 30 seconds

Start in a high plank pose with your hands directly under your shoulders and a dumbbell placed behind your right hand. If this is too hard, put your knees on the ground. Engage your core as you use your left hand to lift the dumbbell across your body to the left side. Do not let your body twist or your hips dip. Imagine balancing a cup of hot coffee on your back as you repeat, side to side, for 30 seconds. A wider stance with your feet will provide more stability.

Short-Lever Side Plank and Leg Lift

Purpose: Strengthens the side muscles, abdominals, glutes and shoulders

Time: 30 seconds per side

Start in a modified side plank: Place your right forearm on the floor and bend your right knee at a 90-degree angle, tucking your heel behind you. Keep your hips stacked as you extend your top leg. Your foot can rest on the floor for balance and your left hand can rest on your top hip or over your belly. Raise your hips off the ground, supporting yourself on right leg. Engage your core as you lift your leg just above hip level, then lower it so the inside of your foot taps the ground. Repeat for 30 seconds, then switch sides.

Plank Walk-Ups

Purpose: Strengthens the deep abdominals, particularly the deep core, shoulder stabilizers and deep back muscles

Time: 30 seconds

Start in a high plank pose with your hands directly under your shoulders. If this is too hard, put your knees on the ground. Engage your core as you drop down to a forearm plank, leading with your right hand. Press back up to plank pose, first straightening your right arm, then your left. One the next round, drop down and press up with your left side first. Continue alternating sides. If you are in a full plank, don’t let your hips rotate or sag.
