Who’s Winning the Race for Congress?

“It’s a five-alarm fire.” A “slow moving car crash.” Republicans are “hemorrhaging” and “should brace for losing everything.”

Republicans have been full of doom in recent days, as they confront the reality that their political prospects do not seem to be improving. And their candidates are rapidly running out of time for a turnaround.

With less than 30 days before the election, Democrats are reaching deeper into traditionally conservative areas while Republicans find themselves fighting to save their incumbents.

Decisions last week by Republicans to pull money out of the North Carolina Senate race and divert resources to Kansas demonstrated the depth of their party’s challenge, as new New York Times/Siena University polls found strength for Democrats in five reliably Republican states that could determine control of the chamber.

Based on New York Times/Siena polls of 504 voters in Alaska conducted Sept. 24 to Oct. 1, 606 voters in Iowa conducted Sept. 22 to Oct. 1, 605 voters in Kansas conducted Sept. 22 to 30, 616 voters in Ohio conducted Sept. 22 to Oct. 1 and 615 voters in Texas conducted Sept. 21 to 30.

Jillian Vordick/The New York Times

The first events in what President Trump has promised will be a series of midterm appearances — all in deep red states — underscored the anxiety among Republicans that even some of their most loyal voters could stay home from the polls in November.

Data is as of Sept. 25, 2026. Note: To gain control of the Senate, Democrats would need to win 51 seats to overcome Vice President JD Vance's tie-breaking vote for Republicans.

Source: The Cook Political Report Senate race ratings
