ARTICLE

Judge Blocks Border Wall Construction in Big Bend in Texas
The ruling dealt a setback to the Trump administration’s plans for hundreds of miles of barriers that have drawn local opposition.
By J. David Goodman
Published 2026-10-02 20:32 UTC
https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/02/us/border-wall-big-bend-texas.html

A federal judge in El Paso on Friday halted border wall construction across the Big Bend region, dealing a blow to the Trump administration’s effort to erect hundreds of miles of barriers despite fierce local opposition.

The federal government “shall not build border barrier or related infrastructure, or engage in any construction or ground disturbing activities, in the Big Bend sector,” the judge, Kathleen Cardone, wrote in her order, which she read from the bench.

In a two-page order granting an injunction in the case, the judge said the federal government could not rely on waivers of environmental and other laws it had granted itself to speed up the construction, writing that it “must comply with all applicable laws.” Judge Cardone ruled that the government could not use the waivers until the case was resolved.

She wrote that the plaintiffs were likely to succeed on the merits of at least one of their claims.

The lawsuit, filed in the Western District of Texas by plaintiffs including a river boat guide and a church preservation group, sought to stop U.S. Customs and Border Protection from building border barriers across a more-than-500-mile stretch of the frontier with Mexico. The plans, which include some barriers in Big Bend National Park, have been the focus of months of protests and stop-and-start construction activity.

In a statement, the border protection agency said that it was working “on next steps” with the Justice Department. “We stand by our assessment and remain confident in our legal authorities to secure the border while preserving the unique landscape of the Big Bend region,” the statement said.

The order came days after federal lawyers told the court in a legal filing that the border protection agency would resume barrier construction in several areas in the Big Bend region, though not the national park, after a voluntary pause on activity. The agency also said in the filing that it was revising its plans for the national park — where bulldozers and heavy machinery had already begun operating in August — and would make them public early this month.

Outside the courthouse, river guides and environmental activists cheered after the order was issued.

“We dodged a bullet today,” said Billy Miller, a river guide who lives in Terlingua, Texas, and was one of the plaintiffs in the suit. “It means that we can continue to introduce people to the wildness. Their plan was going to ruin the experience of anybody going out there.”

The opposition to border construction in the Big Bend region of Texas, a remote area known for its rugged wilderness, included national groups and local residents. Among the arguments made by the plaintiffs was that few illegal crossings take place in the area because of the inherent dangers posed by its remoteness. The Big Bend sector records far fewer crossings than any other portion of the Texas-Mexico border.

Federal officials have argued that barrier construction was necessary, even if crossings were currently low, because the number would increase as barriers were constructed in other parts of the border and migrants looked for new ways to get into the country.

Still, opponents considered the ruling as a significant victory.

“No construction can continue,” said Laiken Jordahl, who was in the courtroom on Friday and is an advocate with the Center for Biological Diversity, an environmental nonprofit that is one of the plaintiffs. “All the bulldozers have been stopped across the entire border sector.”
