ARTICLE

Here’s Senator Cotton’s Cell Number, Trump Says, in Dispute Over Daylight Saving Bill
The president urged people to call Senator Tom Cotton of Arkansas and accused him of blocking a bill to cement daylight saving time.
By Mack Liederman and Robert Jimison
Published 2026-10-04 00:02 UTC
https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/03/us/trump-tom-cotton-cellphone-number.html

President Trump wants to make daylight saving time permanent. Senator Tom Cotton does not. On Saturday, that disagreement led the president to share what he said was the Arkansas Republican’s cellphone number on social media, urging the state’s residents to take the issue up with Mr. Cotton personally.

Mr. Trump, championing the Sunshine Protection Act, which has been stalled in the Senate since July, accused Mr. Cotton of holding up the bill in a lengthy Truth Social post. Then Mr. Trump shared 10 digits.

“To the Great People of Arkansas, please let your Senator know it would be a GREAT Bipartisan WIN for the United States Senate,” Mr. Trump said. “Call Tom on his cellphone.”

Two officials confirmed that the number Mr. Trump gave out belonged to Mr. Cotton, whose office did not respond to a request for a comment. The officials spoke on the condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to discuss the matter publicly. Calls went to an automated voice mail greeting saying an unnamed recipient was “temporarily unavailable.”

The bill, which would cement daylight saving time and stop Americans from having to change their clocks twice a year, has been stuck in the Senate after sailing through the House in July with a 308-to-117 vote.

Year-round daylight-saving time was last tried in 1974, but public sentiment quickly soured as Americans complained of dark winter mornings, particularly for parents taking children to school. It was repealed in less than a year.

But Mr. Trump has described moving clocks an hour forward in the spring and an hour back in the fall as “ridiculous.” On Saturday, he told reporters that the practice “discombobulates everybody.”

His post directed at Mr. Cotton included a letter from Brian Rolapp, the chief executive of the PGA Tour, endorsing the bill, which would add an extra hour of daylight in the evening. Mr. Rolapp claimed that would allow golfers to squeeze in 23 million more rounds a year.

Many sleep scientists think the current practice is better because it means more sunshine in the morning, in sync with the body’s internal clock.

Later on Saturday, Mr. Cotton stood firm on his position but did not mention Mr. Trump’s post. The two Republicans have normally been allies.

“No one likes changing the clock, me included, but Congress has enacted permanent Daylight Savings Time multiple times before and repealed it each time once Americans experienced absurdly late sunrises in the winter,” Mr. Cotton said in a social media post. “Arkansans don’t want their kids going to school in the pitch dark.”

The president responded within the hour. “It has been held up in the Senate, a place where things go to die, by Tom Cotton, for months,” Mr. Trump said of the bill. “And for what, other than ‘ego?’”

It is not the first time the president has shared a phone number with the intention of pressuring a senator. During his run in the 2016 Republican presidential primary, Mr. Trump flaunted a card onstage with a handwritten number for Senator Lindsey Graham of South Carolina, a rival for the nomination. He read the digits aloud and encouraged Americans to give the senator a call.

Speaking at Mr. Graham’s funeral in July, Mr. Trump said that sharing the number was something he “shouldn’t have done.”

“And I remember the number,” Mr. Trump said, reciting the digits again at the funeral. “If anybody wants to give it a shot,” he added.
