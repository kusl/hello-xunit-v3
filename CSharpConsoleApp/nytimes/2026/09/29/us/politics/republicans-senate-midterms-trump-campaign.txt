ARTICLE

In Nine Key Senate Races, Five Republicans Won’t Commit to Campaigning With Trump
Three campaigns left their positions unclear, and two said they would not join the president on the trail.
By Tim Balk
Published 2026-09-29 17:40 UTC
https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/29/us/politics/republicans-senate-midterms-trump-campaign.html

President Trump has said he plans to campaign for dozens of Republicans running in competitive races in the lead-up to the midterm election on Nov. 3.

Mr. Trump has several stops scheduled in the coming days, including visits to heavily Republican states such as Oklahoma, Alabama and Tennessee. But the president is deeply unpopular, and some Republicans in battleground states have started to separate themselves from his administration, his costly war with Iran and his polarizing immigration policies.

For Republicans running in the most competitive Senate races, Mr. Trump can pose a dilemma. His super PAC is flush with cash that could help them, and he remains popular with the Republican base. But he could be toxic with swing voters.

Among the nine Republicans whose races are expected to be crucial in determining Senate control, four would like Mr. Trump to join them on the trail ahead of the election, or are open to the possibility, according to their campaigns. Three campaigns left their positions unclear, and two said they would not join the president on the trail.

Here’s where the candidates stand on whether they would campaign with Mr. Trump in their home states. (These nine candidates are running in races that the nonpartisan Cook Political Report rates as the most competitive: tossups and races leaning toward Democrats or Republicans.)

Yes

Senator Dan Sullivan of Alaska

Mr. Sullivan has “every intention” of campaigning with Mr. Trump in Alaska at some point in October, said a campaign spokesman, Nate Adams. Mr. Trump said at a news conference this month that he would visit conservative-leaning Alaska in the last 30 days of the campaign.

Plans for a visit have not been firmed up, Mr. Adams said.

Mr. Sullivan is trying to fend off former Representative Mary Peltola, an independent-minded Democrat who finished first in the all-party primary in August.

Michael Whatley of North Carolina

Perhaps more than any Republican running in a competitive Senate race, Mr. Whatley has stood by Mr. Trump. Two weeks ago, the two campaigned together in Gastonia, N.C., near Charlotte, where Mr. Whatley declared, “I am still with that guy,” referring to the president.

A spokesman for Mr. Whatley, DJ Griffin, said the candidate would welcome further opportunities to campaign with the president.

Mr. Whatley faces former Gov. Roy Cooper, a popular Democrat, in a swing state that Mr. Trump carried narrowly in 2024. Mr. Cooper leads in the polls.

Senator Jon Husted of Ohio

Mr. Husted is scheduled to be joined by Mr. Trump at a rally on Saturday in Vandalia, which is north of Dayton. “We look forward to hosting President Trump in Ohio this weekend,” Amy Natoce, a campaign spokeswoman, said in a statement.

Mr. Husted is trying to fend off Sherrod Brown, a Democratic former senator and longtime fixture of Ohio politics.

Ken Paxton of Texas

Mr. Paxton, the scandal-plagued Texas attorney general, has aligned himself closely with Mr. Trump, campaigning with the president more than once this year and speaking at Mr. Trump’s midterm convention this month.

A spokesman for Mr. Paxton, Nick Maddux, said in a text message that Mr. Paxton would “of course” be open to campaigning with Mr. Trump again in the lead-up to the election.

Mr. Trump is visiting Denton, Texas, on Thursday for an event focused on his economic agenda, said Olivia Wales, a White House spokeswoman. Mr. Paxton will be in West Texas for previously scheduled events, including a rally in Amarillo, Mr. Maddux said.

Mr. Paxton is in a tight race with James Talarico, a Democratic state lawmaker and seminarian.

No

Senator Susan Collins of Maine

Ms. Collins, a centrist who has had a turbulent relationship with Mr. Trump, has long ruled out campaigning with him in her Democratic-leaning state.

“That’s just not going to happen” Ms. Collins said in an interview in June, adding, “I’m happy running my own campaign.”

Ms. Collins is running against Troy Jackson, a Democratic former state lawmaker who was once was president of the Maine Senate.

John E. Sununu of New Hampshire

Mr. Sununu’s campaign has signaled he has no interest in being joined by Mr. Trump on the trail in Democratic-leaning New Hampshire.

“John’s focus the entire campaign has been on New Hampshire,” Mike Schrimpf, a campaign spokesman, said in a statement, adding that Mr. Sununu “doesn’t plan to campaign with anyone from outside New Hampshire.”

Mr. Sununu is running against Representative Chris Pappas, a four-term Democrat.

Unclear or no comment

Mike Rogers of Michigan

Mr. Rogers, who joined two rallies with the president during the summer, is walking a general-election tightrope in Michigan, a swing state that went narrowly for Mr. Trump in 2024.

A spokeswoman for Mr. Rogers, Abby Ronson, said the campaign had no comment on whether Mr. Rogers would be open to campaigning with Mr. Trump before the general election.

Mr. Rogers faces Dr. Abdul El-Sayed, a progressive Democrat running as a populist outsider.

Representative Ashley Hinson of Iowa

Ms. Hinson received Mr. Trump’s endorsement for Senate last September, early in the election cycle, and defended him earlier in 2025 at testy town halls where she was heckled. She also appeared with Mr. Trump in the Oval Office on Monday, where she expressed appreciation for his agenda.

At the same time, Ms. Hinson, whose race is considered a tossup, has sought distance from Mr. Trump and called for an “immediate” end to the Iran war.

At the White House, Mr. Trump told Ms. Hinson that he planned to campaign with her at a “great rally” in Iowa. Ms. Hinson, nodding, replied, “We’ll save you a good Iowa pork chop, sir.”

Still, Ms. Hinson’s representatives did not respond to numerous requests for comment on whether she would be open to campaigning with Mr. Trump. When Vice President JD Vance visited the state for a campaign rally this month, Ms. Hinson did not join him.

She is running against Josh Turek, a Democratic state lawmaker.

Senator Roger Marshall of Kansas

Mr. Marshall, a first-term senator, has generally been a reliable defender of Mr. Trump, braving boos to defend the president’s tariffs during a candidate’s debate this month. He also appeared with Mr. Vance at an event in the Kansas City area this month.

But Mr. Marshall’s campaign did not respond to requests for comment on whether he would be open to campaigning with Mr. Trump before the election.

Mr. Marshall is trying to fend off the Rev. Adam Hamilton, a Democrat, in a traditionally conservative state. Kansas’s agriculture-heavy economy has been hit hard by the war with Iran and the administration’s tariff policies.
