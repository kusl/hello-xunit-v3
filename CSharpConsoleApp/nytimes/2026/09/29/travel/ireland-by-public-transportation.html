By Elaine Glusac

Visuals by Michael Vince Kim

Published Sept. 29, 2026Updated Oct. 5, 2026, 5:33 p.m. ET

Boarding the public bus that follows the coast of Ireland’s Dingle Peninsula, I pointed to Coumeenoole Beach on the map and asked the driver to let me off there.

“Certainly,” he smiled, “but only if you can pronounce it.”

I got a laugh for attempting COO-men-oohl, before disembarking near a pocket strand hugged by cliffs and bashed by waves. It was a setting in David Lean’s 1970 film “Ryan’s Daughter,” but nowadays a nearby sign reads “Star Wars Beach,” presumably because parts of “Star Wars: The Last Jedi” were filmed in the area.

The wind blew strongly, as it does here. A light rain fell, not uncommon in the fall. And the bus, as rural buses are known to do, wouldn’t return for hours, forcing my sister and me, after chasing waves on the deserted beach, to walk for miles to reach shelter.

A waterproof jacket should be on your packing list during most seasons in oft-soggy Ireland, but especially when you’re traveling car free.

In addition to being a more sustainable and social mode of travel, public transportation in Ireland means you don’t have to attempt to drive your car on the left. Roads can be narrow, lined with hedges and, in my experience, occasionally full of sheep.
