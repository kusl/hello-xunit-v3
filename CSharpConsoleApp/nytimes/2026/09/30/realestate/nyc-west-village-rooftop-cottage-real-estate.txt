ARTICLE

In the West Village, a Rooftop ‘Cottage’ Is on the Market
The asking price for the unique property, which sits atop a six-story brick co-op in a former warehouse, is $10.5 million.
By Vivian Marino
Published 2026-09-30 09:02 UTC
https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/30/realestate/nyc-west-village-rooftop-cottage-real-estate.html

The longtime home of David Puchkoff and Eileen Stukane has the feel of a country house in the middle of nowhere. A screen door off a kitchenette opens to a cozy wooden porch with a swing and a large table for family meals. It looks out onto a dwarf Japanese maple tree and a garden of flowering sedums, fragrant herbs and vegetables.

And then there are the views of Midtown Manhattan, which is only about a 20-minute subway ride away.

The unique property in the heart of the West Village sits atop a six-story brick building, a former warehouse that was built around 1906. Mr. Puchkoff, a developer, bought the building in 1974 for about $220,000, and then converted it into a 12-unit co-op and kept a penthouse for himself. In the early 2000s, he added an irrigated, roughly 1,100-square-foot green roof and a cottage-like structure with two paned windows and a porch that connected to the apartment below.

The bucolic home, which has gained widespread publicity over the years, is now on the market. The asking price is $10.5 million, according to Jim St. André of Compass, who is listing the penthouse with Michael Maniawski and Trevor Stephens. Monthly maintenance is $5,468.

“It’s an interesting juxtaposition between country and urban,” Mr. St. André said of the property, adding that outdoor residential space in the West Village is uncommon. “You have this cottage in one world and in the background the lights of New York City. We’re not in Kansas anymore, but we pretty much are.”

Mr. Puchkoff was inspired to build the green rooftop and porch after visiting a friend’s lakefront house in rural Pennsylvania. Installing it all was an arduous endeavor that involved, among other things, hiring a structural engineer and using a tube alongside the building to blow soil onto the roof. While he and Ms. Stukane have enjoyed their private oasis — they were married in the home’s living room in 1988 — both say it’s time to move on.

“I think as we’re aging, a lot of our attention is redirected now to our children and our grandchildren,” said Mr. Puchkoff, who is 87 and now retired. The couple, who have three daughters, said they might stay in Manhattan or move to the Hudson Valley (where porches are ubiquitous). “Right now, it’s all in flux,” he said.

“We think of it as an adventure,” Ms. Stukane said.

The couple’s sixth-floor apartment, which encompasses around 3,000 square feet, has four bedrooms and two full bathrooms. Ms. Stukane, a writer, has used one of the bedrooms as an office.

Exposed wood columns, girders and brick are throughout the unit, as are oak floors, high ceilings and an abundance of storage space. Windows provide extensive views of Midtown and Lower Manhattan.

Off the elevator, the entry is through a foyer with several adjacent closets and Ms. Stukane’s office. A hallway with a bathroom leads to a loft-like space that includes a living room, a dining area and an open kitchen.

The heart of the home is the living room, which is anchored by a wood-burning fireplace that was crafted from discarded cobblestone that Mr. Puchkoff said he had found “all over the streets in the ’70s.” Near the center of the space is a nautical-style, stainless-steel staircase with cruise ship vibes; it rises to the rooftop cottage-like structure, which has a galley-like kitchenette.

“This is a nod to our sailing days,” Ms. Stukane said.

The main kitchen features butcher block countertops, a mahogany breakfast bar and oak cabinets. It is equipped with stainless-steel appliances and a pantry. Nearby is Mr. Puchkoff’s workroom, a laundry room and the primary bedroom, which has a hot tub in the en suite bathroom, as well as views of Hudson Yards.

At the other side of the apartment are two more bedrooms, one of which is a loft-style room up a short set of stairs.

Mr. Puchkoff and Ms. Stukane said they would miss the penthouse, and all the parties and dinners they hosted in the outdoor space.

“The porch has been a gathering place for family,” Ms. Stukane said. “It made us very popular with our relatives.”

They also have fond memories of gardening. “I’m the herb and vegetable gardener,” Ms. Stukane said. “We really do well with eggplant, zucchini and lettuce. And there are lots of tomatoes and a big section of basil.”

Neither is sad, though, about leaving. “We saw this area evolve over the last 50 years from a wild, unruly area to what it is today,” said Mr. Puchkoff, who developed several residential properties in the neighborhood.

Of his most memorable project, Mr. Puchkoff said, “I had the good fortune to be part of this garden and now it’s up to someone else to do what they want with it.”
