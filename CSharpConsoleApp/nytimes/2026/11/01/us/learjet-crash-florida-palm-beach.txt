ARTICLE

What to Know About the Florida Plane Crash That Killed 9
A small jet plummeted to the ground on Sunday, exploding into a fireball and killing everyone on board. The authorities did not immediately release information about the victims.
By Bernard Mokam, Christina Morales, Georgia Gee and David Ovalle
Published 2026-11-02 02:50 UTC
https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/01/us/learjet-crash-florida-palm-beach.html

The authorities are investigating why a private jet carrying nine people plunged from the sky on Sunday afternoon, falling at a rate of 20,000 feet per minute before crashing into a roadway outside Palm Beach, Fla.

Everyone on board was killed, the authorities said. They have not released information about the victims. One of the pilots was a 20-year-old aviator named Nathalia Filgueiras, according to her mother, Camila Corteleti.

The plane, a Learjet 60, was traveling from Austin, Texas, to North Eleuthera Airport in the Bahamas when it crashed in a residential area, showering the neighborhood in debris and forcing the authorities to evacuate hundreds of residents.

Here’s what we know.

What happened?

Radio traffic between the pilot and air traffic control in Miami indicated that the plane was encountering “light, clear ice that is manageable” on Sunday afternoon, as well as “moderate turbulence.”

The pilot asked if she could climb to 37,000 feet, presumably to fly above the turbulent air. But moments later, she declared “mayday” to air traffic controllers, indicating a life-threatening emergency.

The plane was “descending 20,000 feet per minute,” she screamed.

“We’re going to crash into the ground,” she added.

At a news conference on Sunday, Gov. Ron DeSantis of Florida said that footage of the crash, captured on dashboard cameras, showed what “looked like a missile going down.”

The Federal Aviation Administration and the National Transportation Safety Board said they would be investigating. The N.T.S.B. has scheduled a news conference for Monday.

Local officials said on Sunday that they were evacuating about 600 residents in the small community of trailer homes near the impact zone, where smoldering chunks of metal ignited small fires.

No one on the ground was injured, officials said.

Ms. Filgueiras, of Boca Raton, Fla., had logged more than 1,300 hours of flight time and earned her pilot’s license at 17, Ms. Corteleti said. She said her daughter had worked for Upper Jets, a private charter firm based in Fort Lauderdale.

What are experts saying?

The cause of the crash was not immediately clear. Aviation safety experts were studying clues such as the plane’s rapid loss of altitude and the pilot’s mention of ice.

The pilot was indicating that the “plane was uncontrollable,” said Steven Arroyo, a retired United Airlines captain who listened to an audio recording of the pilot’s conversation with air traffic controllers.

Mary Schiavo, an aviation lawyer and former inspector general for the Transportation Department, said that ice buildup on a plane’s wings could disrupt airflow and reduce lift. That could be consistent with how the plane dropped from the sky so suddenly.

“Icing can be so severe it can overcome and stall the plane,” said Ms. Schiavo, who reviewed publicly available flight data and videos posted on social media.

“That is a plane not descending, but a plane falling,” she said.

What did witnesses see?

Esteban Ruiz, a resident of the trailer home community near the crash site, said he had been relaxing on Sunday afternoon when he heard a loud noise.

“I thought maybe it was lightning,” he said, adding that he also heard what he thought was rain pelting his roof. It was actually metal debris from the plane or its impact. Mr. Ruiz said he rushed outside and saw a chaotic scene and a crater in the ground.

Stephanie Platts, who also lived near the crash site, said she had been sitting on her patio with family members when she heard what sounded like a car crash. Ms. Platts said her mobile home caught on fire, caused by flying debris.

Neighbors tried to help her put out the flames. She said she saw human remains scattered around her.

“Everything was engulfed in flames,” Ms. Platts said, “and then after the flames, was like rain of metal.”
