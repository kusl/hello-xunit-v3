ARTICLE

Senator Chris Murphy Is ‘Awake and Alert’ After Suffering a Stroke
The Connecticut senator, who has been campaigning for fellow Democrats, has been viewed as a potential candidate for president in 2028.
By Michael Gold
Published 2026-11-01 17:58 UTC
https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/01/us/politics/chris-murphy-stroke.html

Senator Christopher S. Murphy, Democrat of Connecticut, was hospitalized on Saturday after suffering a stroke, according to a statement from his chief of staff.

The chief of staff, Allison Herwitt, said that Mr. Murphy, 53, was attending his son’s hockey game on Saturday afternoon and “started experiencing numbness in his arm.” He was taken to a nearby hospital, where doctors diagnosed a stroke.

Mr. Murphy was transferred to Johns Hopkins Hospital in Baltimore for monitoring and treatment. “The doctors are encouraged by his progress; he is awake and alert; and we are optimistic he will make a full recovery,” Ms. Herwitt said in her statement.

A spokesman for Mr. Murphy would not provide further details about his condition on Sunday.

Mr. Murphy, who became a senator in 2013 and has been viewed as a potential 2028 presidential candidate, has spent the past several weeks on the campaign trail with Democrats as he tries to help his party take control of Congress in the midterm elections. He had been scheduled to help campaign in New Hampshire on Sunday.

Mr. Murphy is one of several senators to experience significant public health issues in recent years, as the health of politicians has received greater scrutiny. The sudden death of Senator Lindsey Graham, Republican of South Carolina, in July from an aortic dissection, a tear in the body’s main artery, added to those concerns.

At least two of Mr. Murphy’s colleagues have had strokes in recent years. Senator Chris Van Hollen, Democrat of Maryland, experienced what he said was a “minor stroke” in 2022. Senator John Fetterman, Democrat of Pennsylvania, had a near-fatal stroke that same year, as he was campaigning for election.

Senator Mitch McConnell, Republican of Kentucky and the former majority leader, was gone from the Capitol and out of public view for more than three months earlier this year after a health incident at his home.

Mr. McConnell has also appeared to experience medical issues at the Capitol and in Kentucky, freezing during appearances and needing to be escorted away by his aides.
