8 Exercises to Improve Your Grip Strength

This simple routine can help make everyday tasks easier — and you can do it while sitting at your desk.

How often do you call on your grip strength during your average day? Each time you lift a bag of groceries, pull a weed or turn a key in a lock, you’re relying on muscles in your hands and forearms to work in coordination.

“A lot of these things require more strength than you may really notice until you start to lose it,” said Nick Maroldi, a physical therapist and hand therapist at Hospital for Special Surgery in White Plains, New York.

Conditions like arthritis, tendinitis and carpal tunnel syndrome can weaken your grip. So can the muscle loss and joint stiffness of aging, said Michelle Annes, a hand therapist and occupational therapist at Shirley Ryan AbilityLab in Chicago.

A strong grip is linked to longevity and well-being, while a weak grip often accompanies heart disease, cognitive decline and even some types of cancer. Improving your grasp won’t automatically enhance your health or lengthen your life, said Ryan McGrath, an associate professor at North Dakota State University. But bringing grip-focused moves in your routine may improve your quality of life by helping everyday tasks feel easier.

And squeezing a stress ball isn’t the best way to do it, experts said. Repeatedly clenching your hand can worsen or even cause issues like carpal tunnel, trigger finger or tendinitis, Dr. Maroldi said. Instead, work the whole muscle chain that supports your grip: hands, wrists and shoulders.

The following routine designed by Ms. Annes engages all these muscles and takes your wrist joint through its full range of motion, which may prevent or ease some types of pain and stiffness. It’s designed for you to do it your desk, so it can double as a workday break.

Overview

Time: 10 to 15 minutes

Intensity: Low

Rounds: Perform the recommended number of repetitions with a few seconds’ rest between each exercise. Do them once or twice per day, two to three days per week.

What you’ll need

  • One large rubber band — the thicker or stiffer, the more challenging

  • Two heavy items of equal weight, such as hand weights, full water bottles or tote bags filled with books

Adjust for you

If you don’t have much experience with strength training, skip the desktop push-ups and farmer’s hold. Stop any movement that causes you pain in your hands or wrists.

Scapular Squeezes

Targets: Back and shoulder muscles

Repetitions: Five

Just as your core supports your posture, your shoulders stabilize your arm position, creating a solid foundation for your grip, Ms. Annes said.

You can do this exercise standing or seated. If you are sitting, keep your feet flat on the floor, your hips at 90 degrees to your torso and your knees bent at 90 degrees. Let your hands rest in your lap, so you move only your shoulders, not your arms. Gently pull your shoulders back and down, squeezing your shoulder blades together. Hold for one inhale and one exhale, then release.

Wrist Flexor Stretch

Targets: Muscles on the underside of your forearm

Repetitions: Five on each side, 10 to 30 seconds each

This stretch helps improve the flow of blood and synovial fluid, a natural lubricant that supports joint health.

Begin seated or standing, and extend your right arm straight in front of you, palm facing up. If your shoulder is tight or painful, keep your elbow bent 90 degrees and your upper arm by your side, extending only your forearm in front of you.

Bend your wrist down so your palm is facing away and your fingers are pointing toward the floor. Use your left hand to gently pull your right hand down and back for a deeper stretch. You may feel a gentle stretch on the palm side of your forearm. Make small adjustments to your position if you feel any pain or pinching in your wrist. Repeat on the other side.

Wrist Extensor Stretch

Targets: Muscles on the back of your forearm

Repetitions: Five on each side, 10 to 30 seconds each

Begin in a similar position — seated or standing, with your right arm out in front of you — but with your palm down. Bend your wrist so your fingers are pointing down and your palm is facing toward you. This time you’ll feel the stretch on the back of your forearm. Hold there, or use your left hand to pull your hand further down and toward you. Repeat on the other side.

Tendon glides

Targets: Finger flexors and extensors

Repetitions: Five, where one rep is moving through all five positions

Stand or sit next to a desk or bookshelf with your right elbow resting on the surface, hand raised, as if waving. Begin with your wrist neutral and your palm facing away from you. From there, move through four additional hand positions, pausing briefly in each one.

First, keeping your thumb straight, bend the middle and end joints of your other four fingers, so your hand looks like a claw, hook, or the letter “e” in sign language. Next, bring the tops of your fingers to your palm to close your hand into a gentle fist. Next, straighten the middle and end joints to form a tabletop or upside down L. Finally, bend at the middle joint again to make another fist, but with your fingertips resting on your palm. Repeat on the other side.

Finger Abduction With Rubber Band

Targets: Small muscles within the hand

Repetitions: 10 on each hand

Place a rubber band around your fingers, crisscrossing it between each finger. Push your fingers out against the resistance of the band, then release. Repeat with the other hand.

If that’s too hard, try wrapping the band around all your fingers without twisting between. If it’s too easy, add another band, use a thicker one or move it higher up the fingers.

Wrist Circles

Targets: Wrist flexors and extensors, along with stabilizing muscles in the forearm

Repetitions: Two to three sets of 10 to 15 seconds in each direction

This movement is a dynamic stretch for your wrists, increasing the flexibility and mobility of the joint and the surrounding muscles.

Begin either sitting tall in a chair or standing, feet hip-width apart. Bend your elbows and clasp your hands in front of you, fingers interlaced. Circle your wrists clockwise for 10 to 15 seconds, then counterclockwise for the same amount of time.

Desk Push-Ups

Targets: Chest, triceps, shoulders, abs and back

Repetitions: Eight to 10

Stand with your hands on your desk, your feet out behind you — the farther back they are, the more challenging the exercise. Slowly lower yourself to your desk as far as is comfortable, not allowing your elbows to bend any more than 90 degrees. On an exhale, push back up and straighten your elbows to return to the starting position.

Farmer’s Hold

Targets: Forearms, upper back, shoulders, lower back and core

Repetitions: Two, 30 seconds to a minute each

Pick up two heavy objects of equal weight — such as weights, full water bottles or bags filled with books — one in each hand. The objects should be heavy enough that you feel your arm and shoulder muscles engaging, but not so heavy that you worry you’ll drop them. Stand tall for 30 seconds to one minute, keeping your shoulders down and back, your core engaged and your arms hanging by your sides.
