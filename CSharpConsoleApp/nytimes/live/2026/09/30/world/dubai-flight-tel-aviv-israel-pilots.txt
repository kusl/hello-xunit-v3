Here’s the latest.

One pilot stabbed another at the controls of an Emirati flight to Tel Aviv on Wednesday, causing it to suddenly plunge thousands of feet until passengers and the crew won a fight to regain control of the plane.

Passengers described a terrifying fall during the attack, which ended when some of them stepped into the melee and managed to subdue the aggressive pilot. Overhead bins swung open, parents clung to their children, and videos from inside the plane showed at least one wounded man and others on board covered in blood.

Crew members and passengers made an emergency landing in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia, the Israeli prime minister said, and all of the passengers arrived safely in Israel by Wednesday night, after a replacement aircraft was sent to retrieve them by FlyDubai, the airline operating the original flight. As they disembarked, they shared harrowing stories of what happened on board.

A dentist, Shota Musev, said he was one of the first to reach the cockpit after the attack, finding the injured pilot thrown to the ground there. He said he treated him in midair, including for severe wounds to his head and cuts on his fingers.

Those in the cabin recalled fear racing through them. “In my head, I said, ‘I’m so screwed, I’m so screwed. I don’t deserve to die like this,’” said Peleg Itali, 16, a high school student who was on the plane.

And Asaf Rajuan, one of the passengers who raced to the cockpit, said he and five others “acted on instinct” to restrain the attacker as they realized the plane was out of control.

The plane nose-dived for what felt like 10 to 15 seconds, he said, adding that the passengers’ only hope of rescuing the plane was to work together. Without his fellow passengers, he said, “we’d never have survived.”

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said in a video statement that one pilot had stabbed the other and “apparently attempted to crash the plane with its passengers on board,” nearly all of them Israelis.

He called the pilot who was stabbed, whom he identified as Capt. Smit Machchhar, a hero who saved the lives of 174 people.

“Despite being stabbed and seriously injured, he fought back, resisted, opened the cockpit door, and enabled passengers and crew to overpower the attacker — preventing a catastrophic midair disaster,” he said.

Much about the episode, including the cause of the struggle in the cockpit, remains unclear. The Israeli authorities were investigating whether it was an attempted attack on Israelis or a suicide attempt, among other possible explanations, according to several Israeli officials, all of whom spoke on condition of anonymity in accordance with government protocol.

A FlyDubai spokesperson said only that an altercation broke out and that crew members who were on duty secured the aircraft.

The aircraft sent distress signals and abruptly reversed course near the Saudi-Jordanian border before making an emergency landing at an airport in Tabuk at about 9:45 a.m. local time. Israeli officials said the plane was carrying 160 to 180 passengers.

The pilot and co-pilot were hospitalized in Saudi Arabia after sustaining injuries, according to a statement from officials at the Tabuk airport. Three Israeli security sources, speaking on the condition of anonymity to comply with government protocol, said the pilot who they believed had carried out the aggression was a citizen of Oman. The Omani foreign ministry declined to immediately comment.

Passengers describe panic and heroics after the cockpit stabbing.

Passengers on a morning flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv recounted scenes of panic and terror on Wednesday as their aircraft suddenly plunged thousands of feet while screams for help came from behind the cockpit door.

The Israeli prime minister, Benjamin Netanyahu, said one of the pilots had stabbed the other, Capt. Smit Machchhar, an Indian citizen, and then had tried to crash the plane. The identity of the pilot who attacked Captain Machchhar has not yet been released.

What began as a routine flight became terrifying when the violent altercation broke out inside the cockpit. Passengers, most of them Israelis, described a desperate scramble inside the cabin, as travelers realized the plane was out of control.

A few rushed into the cockpit and tackled the pilot who had stabbed his colleague before two off-duty pilots, who had been traveling in the main cabin, helped land the aircraft safely in Saudi Arabia.

Meital Zoller, who was traveling with her husband and child, said they realized something was wrong when “the plane went up and down.” Then they heard screams, and the plane began diving.

“Within a few minutes, our plane completely lost control, and we felt that this was it — we were about to crash,” one passenger, Miriam Ohayon, told Israel’s I24 News, a television channel. “We heard screams on the plane, and when they opened the cockpit, there was a lot of blood.”

Asaf Rajuan, one of the Israeli passengers who helped subdue the attacker, told reporters that he and five other passengers “acted on instinct” after realizing that the plane was in a steep nosedive. They burst into the cockpit and wrestled with the pilot who had attacked his colleague.

“We neutralized him very quickly,” he said, adding that crew members helped restrain him.

Dr. Shota Musev, a dentist who said he was one of the first to reach the cockpit after the attack, said that he and another passenger tied the attacker’s hands together with headphone cables.

Dr. Musev said he treated the injured pilot for severe wounds to his head and cuts on his fingers. He said the attacker’s body was bloody as well, but it was not immediately clear if the blood was his or someone else’s.

Hila Gaier, who was also on the flight, said that when the plane dived many passengers couldn’t move because of the forces acting on their bodies. “You were sucked downward,” she told Kan, Israel’s public broadcaster. She said she was flying with her partner and two daughters. “I embraced them, told them I loved them, and we tried to practice breathing exercises,” she said.

Flight-tracking data from Flightradar24 showed Flight 1073 descended more than 17,000 feet in less than two minutes. “The plane kept stabilizing and then dropping again for several long seconds,” one passenger, Almog Itali, told Channel 12. The plane landed safely in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia, and the pilots were taken to a hospital.

Adam Polovincik, 40, who was on the flight with his family, described it as an “extreme experience,” even as he tried to remain calm for his children.

Speaking by phone from Saudi Arabia, which has no formal diplomatic relations with Israel, Mr. Polovincik said the circumstances in the Saudi airport were surreal.

“They brought us food and drink, served us coffee,” Mr. Polovincik said. “Yes, there are a lot of soldiers here and government officials, but the atmosphere is very relaxed, pleasant and respectful.”

The passengers were later flown on a replacement plane to Ben Gurion Airport near Tel Aviv, where they were greeted with Israeli flags. Many said they were still reeling from the ordeal. Peleg Itali, a high school student, described his thoughts as he felt the plane start to plummet.

“In my head, I said, ‘I’m so screwed, I’m so screwed. I don’t deserve to die like this,’” he said.

Israel and Saudi Arabia don’t have formal ties, but Israelis have occasionally landed there.

The flight to Israel that was diverted Wednesday morning after a clash in the cockpit landed safely in Saudi Arabia, a country that Israeli citizens rarely set foot in.

The two nations don’t have formal diplomatic, trade or tourism ties, though attempts to forge them have been central to U.S. regional diplomacy in recent years.

Still, their proximity has taken Israelis to Saudi Arabia under exceptional circumstances.

The 1944 Convention on International Civil Aviation, known as the Chicago Convention, laid the foundation for how global civil aviation is governed. It states that all parties to the agreement, which include Israel and Saudi Arabia, should assist aircraft in distress in their territory.

After Wednesday’s emergency landing at the Tabuk airport, a civil-military facility in northwestern Saudi Arabia, the Saudi authorities said that they would provide “necessary care” to passengers. Their statement did not mention the flight’s connection to Israel. The FlyDubai aircraft had been flying from Dubai to Tel Aviv.

In the past few years, at least two other passenger flights en route to Tel Aviv have made emergency landings in Saudi Arabia.

In September 2025, a flight from Dubai landed in the kingdom because of a medical emergency on board, according to a statement by the Israeli Foreign Ministry carried by Israeli news media. The plane then continued on to its destination, according to the statement, which did not say which airline operated the flight or where it landed in Saudi Arabia.

In 2023, an Air Seychelles flight from the Seychelles, an island nation in the Indian Ocean, landed in Jeddah because of a technical malfunction, according to the government-funded Seychelles News Agency. The airport said at the time that roughly 130 passengers were staying at an airport hotel.

In a statement at the time, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu of Israel thanked the Saudi authorities for their “good neighborliness” and “warm attitude” toward the Israeli passengers.

FlyDubai operates up to 10 daily flights between Dubai and Tel Aviv.

FlyDubai became the first airline offering commercial flights between Dubai and Tel Aviv in 2020 after Israel and the United Arab Emirates signed an agreement to normalize relations.

The new route meant that Israeli passengers could catch connecting flights in the Persian Gulf, rather than having to go via Europe, significantly cutting down on travel time.

FlyDubai operated the first scheduled commercial flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv in November 2020, after Israel and the Emirates signed the U.S.-brokered Abraham Accords, strengthening economic, political and security relations.

A low-cost carrier, FlyDubai initially offered two daily flights from Dubai International Airport to Tel Aviv and now operates up to 10. One of those flights made an emergency landing in Saudi Arabia on Wednesday after what passengers described as a midair altercation between the pilots, which the Israeli authorities were still investigating.

The airline, which began flying in 2009, is wholly owned by Dubai through the Investment Corporation of Dubai, the government’s main investment arm, which also owns Emirates Airlines.

All of the flights between Dubai and Israel travel through Saudi airspace. On Aug. 31, 2020, an El Al jet carrying American and Israeli officials flew to Abu Dhabi along nearly the whole length of Saudi Arabia, the first time Riyadh had allowed an Israeli passenger plane to cross its skies. Two days later, Saudi Arabia said flights from any country to and from the Emirates could fly over its territory, without naming Israel. Saudi Arabia does not have diplomatic relations with Israel.

Most FlyDubai flights fly out of Dubai International Airport’s Terminal 2. Passengers bound for Tel Aviv check in from the much larger Terminal 3, which has heightened security, and can add roughly an hour to the process.

Etihad Airways, Abu Dhabi’s main carrier, added a sixth daily flight to Tel Aviv this summer, making the route its busiest weekly route worldwide.

A passenger describes the chaos of the FlyDubai flight and fears that it would crash.

A passenger on the FlyDubai flight where a clash in the cockpit set off fears of a hijacking Wednesday morning described chaos on board and said that she and others on the plane had thought that it might crash.

The passenger, Miriam Ohayon, spoke to an Israeli television station in one of the first detailed witness accounts to emerge from the plane.

Passengers felt the jet lose control, Ms. Ohayon told Israel’s Channel 24, but some of them intervened to take control. The plane, Flight 1073, which was flying from Dubai to Tel Aviv, landed safely in Saudi Arabia.

“We heard screams on the plane, and when they opened the cockpit, there was a lot of blood,” she said.

Ms. Ohayon’s mother, Yasmin Ohayon, told The New York Times that a woman sitting next to her daughter was among the first to realize something was wrong. The woman saw people fighting inside the cockpit as she was returning from the bathroom and alerted other passengers, Yasmin Ohayon said at Ben Gurion Airport in Tel Aviv, where families were gathering to wait for the passengers.

“Within a few minutes, our plane completely lost control, and we felt that this was it — we were about to crash,” the younger Ms. Ohayon said, adding that she began reciting a Jewish prayer often said in times of distress.

“I’ve already lost my brother; I can’t do this to my mother,” she said she thought. Her brother, Evyatar Ohayon-Abuksis, a soldier, was killed in the Oct. 7, 2023, Hamas-led attack on Israel.

She said that passengers had subdued the attacker and that a nurse and doctor on board provided initial medical care to the pilot.

Asked how the passengers had stopped the attacker, Ms. Ohayon said, “I believe with their bare hands inside the cockpit.”
