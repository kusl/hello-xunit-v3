Tenerife player Jesus de Miguel dies in hospital after on-pitch collision

De Miguel playing for Castellon, who he joined Tenerife from in 2025. Diego Souto/Getty Images

By Nnamdi Onyeagwara
Nov. 3, 2026 Updated 6:45 am EST

CD Tenerife striker Jesus de Miguel has died in hospital after colliding with an opponent during a Spanish second-division soccer match.

The collision between De Miguel, 30, and FC Andorra ​goalkeeper ⁠Jesus Owono led to Monday’s Segunda Division game in Tenerife being abandoned in the sixth minute of second-half added time.

Advertisement

Tenerife said later on Monday that De Miguel was in a “serious but stable” condition, having been taken to the ICU at the local University Hospital of Nuestra Senora de Candelaria. In an update at 3.56am local time on Tuesday morning, the club said that his condition had deteriorated.

Later on Tuesday morning, Tenerife, alongside the Spanish Footballers’ Association (AFE), announced that De Miguel had died at 8am local time.

“Heartbroken, with a lump in our throats and unable to believe this is true, we at CD Tenerife announce the tragic passing of Jesus de Miguel Alameda,” Tenerife said. “There are no words of comfort. There is absolutely nothing that can ease this overwhelming sense of sadness.

“We stand with his devastated family, with his loved ones, and with the entire Tenerife footballing community, who today mourn this cruel turn of events.

“The family of Jesus de Miguel would like to thank everyone for their expressions of support and, from the bottom of their hearts, ask that their privacy be respected at this terrible time.”

A statement from Andorra, who are majority owned by the former Barcelona and Spain defender Gerard Pique, read: “With broken hearts and devastated, we want to send our most sincere condolences to the family of Jesus de Miguel, to all his teammates, friends, and to CD Tenerife. Rest in peace.”

De Miguel’s former clubs CD Castellon and CF Fuenlabrada, as well as Real Madrid and Barcelona, were among the other teams to pay tribute on Tuesday morning.

Advertisement

The Spanish Football Federation (RFEF) later confirmed that men’s and women’s cup games scheduled to take place on Tuesday had been postponed. UEFA, European football’s governing body, said there would be a moment of silence at its Champions League, Europa League, Conference League and Youth League matches taking place this week.

The match between Tenerife and Andorra had originally been scheduled for Sunday evening at 8pm local time, but was moved to Monday afternoon due to heavy rain.

The score was 2-2 at Tenerife’s Estadio Heliodoro Rodriguez Lopez when a clearance put De Miguel through on goal, with Owono rushing out and colliding with the Spaniard. Owono, 25, was shown a straight red card for the foul. Following the collision, medical staff and players from both teams, including Owono, rushed to check on De Miguel, who appeared motionless.

Referee Manuel Angel Perez Hernandez’s match report said: “Given the seriousness of the situation and as there was no sign of improvement in the player’s condition, we withdrew to the changing rooms.

“Once we had met with the captains and team representatives from both sides, we were informed that they were not in the right frame of mind to continue the match. It was therefore decided to call off the match by mutual agreement between all parties.”

Perez Hernandez’s match report also explained why he gave Owono a red card. It said the Equatorial Guinea international had made “a reckless challenge with his leg raised, using excessive force against an opponent whilst the ball was in play”.

‘Shock and pain’

By Spanish football writer Dermot Corrigan

The world of Spanish football was in shock on Tuesday morning when it was confirmed that De Miguel had died.

The depth of the feelings of pain and sympathy was clear as messages came from the clubs he had played with and against as his career progressed upwards through the divisions during over a decade as a professional.

Advertisement

De Miguel came through the youth system at Leganes in his home city of Madrid, then represented other clubs from the Spanish capital including Inter de Madrid, Fuenlabrada, Union Adarve and Navalcarnero in Spain’s third and fourth tiers.

After 18 months at Unionistas de Salamanca, De Miguel’s career took a major step forward in 2023-24 when his 16 goals were crucial in securing promotion to the Segunda Division for Castellon.

After joining Tenerife in summer 2025, the tall No 9’s 11 goals and seven assists in 29 games meant he was central to the joyous celebrations as the Canary Islanders ascended to Spain’s second tier at the end of last season.

A groin injury had hampered his contribution to Tenerife’s 2026-27 campaign, and he had been working his way back to full fitness over recent weeks.
